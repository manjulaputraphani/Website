01 Our roots

Seven seeds. Three centuries.
One promise.

India's coffee story begins in the shade of the Western Ghats, with seven seeds said to have travelled from Yemen to Chikmagalur. We carry that inheritance into every lot we source.

c. 1670

Baba Budan

Legend holds that Baba Budan carried seven coffee seeds from Yemen to the Chandragiri hills. India's coffee story took root in that soil.

Forest

Shade-grown

In Chikmagalur and Coorg, coffee grows slowly under native shade, often beside pepper, cardamom and orange. The forest shapes the cup.

Kaapi

The brass filter

The tumbler and dabarah made coffee a daily South Indian ritual: a strong decoction, hot milk, and a welcome that never feels hurried.

Today

Across the world

We carry that knowledge into every lot we source, sample and ship — to roasters, cafés and importers near and far.

Processing

The process writes the flavour.

Washed

Clean & bright

The fruit is removed, the mucilage fermented and washed away, then the beans are dried. Clarity and acidity come forward.

Cherry→Depulp→Wash→Dry

Natural

Fruit & body

The whole cherry is dried in the sun. The fruit sugars stay close to the bean, bringing sweetness and a fuller body.

Cherry→Dry→Hull→Rest

Monsooned

Mellow & historic

Green beans are exposed to monsoon winds and moisture, swelling and softening. Acidity falls; earth and wood notes rise.

Green bean→Monsoon→Swell→Dry

04 Coffee knowledge

Read the cup
before you buy it.

A shared vocabulary makes a better trade. Here is how we taste, brew and think about the coffee we source.

What to taste

Five things to notice.

01

Acidity

The bright, lively edge of a coffee. It lifts the cup; it should never taste sharp.

02

Sweetness

A natural impression of fruit or sugar, created by the bean, not the spoon.

03

Body

The weight and texture of the coffee on your palate — from silky to syrupy.

04

Bitterness

A natural part of coffee. In balance it adds depth; over-extracted, it turns harsh.

05

Finish

What stays after you swallow: clean, sweet, dry or pleasantly long.

How to taste

Six steps at the cupping table.

  1. 01

    Measure 8.25 g

    Weigh 8.25 g of freshly ground coffee into a cup.

  2. 02

    Pour 150 ml at 93°C

    Pour water at 93°C over the grounds and let the crust form.

  3. 03

    Break at four minutes

    At four minutes, break the crust with a spoon and take in the aroma.

  4. 04

    Skim the surface

    Remove the foam and floating particles so the liquid is clear for tasting.

  5. 05

    Slurp at about 70°C

    Slurp so the coffee spreads across the whole palate and the aroma reaches the nose.

  6. 06

    Score on 100

    Record aroma, flavour, acidity, body and balance. On the SCA scale, 80+ is specialty.

Why coffee

A daily ritual with wider roots.

01

Alertness

Caffeine helps sharpen focus for a few hours by blocking adenosine, the signal that makes us feel sleepy.

02

Antioxidants

Coffee contains chlorogenic acids and other polyphenols, particularly in lightly roasted beans.

03

Long-term health

Moderate coffee drinking has been linked with lower risk of some chronic conditions. The evidence continues to develop.

04

Livelihoods

Coffee supports smallholders and rural communities across the Ghats and beyond. Every fair consignment matters.

A note on caffeine. For most healthy adults, up to about 400 mg a day is considered safe; in pregnancy, about 200 mg. This is general information, not medical advice.
